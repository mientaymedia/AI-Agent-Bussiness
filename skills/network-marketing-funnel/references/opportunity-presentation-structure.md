# Opportunity Presentation Structure

The "Show" step — the actual business-opportunity explanation, adaptable to a live 1:1 conversation, a Zoom group presentation, or a recorded video.

## The Proven Arc

```
1. PROBLEM (2-3 min)
   Name the specific frustration the audience already feels — not a generic
   "want more money" but something concrete. For the default ICP (25-40,
   sales/marketing/SME background — see `academy-icp-and-positioning.md`):
   income capped by hours worked despite already using AI to be more
   efficient at their main job, or seeing AI reshape their industry and
   wanting to turn that into a second income stream rather than just a
   productivity gain for an employer. Ground this in a real, relatable
   scenario — not the generic "trading time for money" framing used for a
   broader audience.

2. INDUSTRY CREDIBILITY (2-3 min)
   Establish that this is a real, large, legitimate industry/company — company
   history, scale, product category credibility. This step exists specifically
   because "MLM" carries baggage; addressing it directly (briefly, not
   defensively) is more effective than avoiding the topic.

3. THE MODEL, EXPLAINED SIMPLY (5-8 min)
   How the business actually works: you personally use/sell product, you build
   a team who also uses/sells product, income comes from product volume (PV/BV)
   not from recruitment itself. Use a simple visual/diagram if presenting live
   or on Zoom — this is the section most prone to sounding confusing if rushed.

4. PROOF (3-5 min)
   Specific, real examples — ideally including your own honest starting point
   and current status, not just a flagship "grand slam" story. A presenter who
   is 3 months in showing modest real progress is often more credible and
   relatable to a similar-stage prospect than only showing a 10-year Diamond's
   result.

5. NEXT STEP (2-3 min)
   One single, clear, low-friction next action — not multiple options. "If this
   resonates, here's exactly what starting looks like: [specific step]." Avoid
   ending on a vague "let me know what you think."
```

**Target total length:** 20-30 minutes live/Zoom, or 12-18 minutes if recorded (recorded video needs tighter pacing since there's no live energy to sustain attention).

## Adapting by Format

| Format | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| 1:1 in-person/call | Most flexible — can adjust pacing live based on questions, spend more time on whichever section the specific prospect needs |
| Live Zoom (group) | Keep strictly to time — group attention drops fast past 30 min; use chat/poll features for engagement in the Problem/Proof sections |
| Recorded video | Front-load Proof earlier (within first 90 seconds) since viewers decide whether to keep watching almost immediately — a strict "Problem then Credibility then Model" order that delays any proof risks losing cold viewers before they see evidence |

## Compliance Checkpoints Within the Presentation

Cross-check against `vietnam-mlm-compliance-basics.md` at each of these specific moments:

- **Proof section**: any number cited must be framed as one individual's specific result, never implied as typical or expected
- **Model section**: must explicitly state income ties to product volume, not recruitment count alone
- **Next Step section**: must not promise or imply a guaranteed outcome from taking the step

## Post-Presentation: Don't Skip Follow-Up

The presentation itself rarely closes a decision on the spot — plan the follow-up touches (2-5 typical) as an explicit part of the funnel, not an afterthought. See `network-marketing-crm`'s prospect check-in cadence for the tracking side of this.

## Usage Example

```
Build a 25-minute Zoom presentation outline for [product category], with my
own starting point as the proof example (3 months in, [specific honest
progress]), following the 5-part arc above.
```
